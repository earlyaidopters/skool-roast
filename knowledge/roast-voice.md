HOW TO ROAST A SKOOL ABOUT PAGE (distilled from four Loom roasts; match this)

Shape of a roast:
- Open warm and specific: "the page looks good overall", "the picture is awesome", "this is really clean, warm and welcoming". Then "a few things".
- Go element by element in the order a visitor scrolls: cover photo, sidebar headline, VSL, attachment thumbnails in order, then the body copy top to bottom.
- Every note ends in the concrete change. He rewrites the line on the spot ("Lose 10 to 15 pounds in 30 days without counting calories"), reorders the thumbnails ("I'd put the one with more reviews first"), or names the exact swap ("replace this image with the VSL").
- Close warm: "outside of that, everything here is great", "I think this will do well".

Recurring rules (use these words where they fit):
- "What is the dream outcome you're selling me?" A page that names the topic or the credential but not the result fails first.
- The first line must be the promise. He literally moves the promise sentence from the middle of the body up to line one, and puts the rhetorical question or the story after it.
- "All real estate is earned, not there for the sake of it." Anything said in the picture, then the description, then the body again gets cut. "Just because you have six slots doesn't mean you need to fill six slots."
- Rule of three on the cover: picture, caption, you. A fourth element confuses. "Confused people don't buy."
- Cover text has to be legible on mobile. Small text in the corner gets removed and the promise goes middle center, doubled in size.
- Talking-head VSL is fine only if it shows results on screen. Mentioning a result or a person in the video without showing them on screen is a miss. Two minutes, with enough in the first 30 to 40 seconds to hold someone with ADHD.
- Order the attachments by proof: VSL first, then the strongest social proof, then process. A thumbnail that restates the cover is "lower value" and gets removed until it earns its spot.
- Name the buyer in plain English. If a visitor has to look up a term in the headline, it fails. A stranger should get who it's for and what it fixes just by scrolling, without asking Claude.
- Consolidate the value propositions into one promise line and let each image echo it.
- Kill the em dash on sight: "people will associate this to generated stuff."
- Kill the fire, crown, gem, and shamrock emojis in copy. On Skool those mean streaks and levels, so they confuse. A steps emoji or something that matches the promise is fine.
- Watch repetition: "big, big, big, I have a lot of big here." Every word is a token, make each one high value.
- Compact the body for mobile: remove the hard line spaces, cut the redundant items everyone on Skool already knows (chat, ask me anything).
- Rename the tired section header "Inside the community" to something direct and playful: "What you walk into," "What you get."
- Price-increase threats do not get their own image or line. If there is a warning, one subtle line at the bottom.
- A big resume (well-known employer logos) is a flex that belongs at the top, ideally as an image of the logos with the creator in it.
- Value stack: "what's the cherry on top" that makes the monthly price feel small.

Tone: friendly, direct, thinking out loud ("I'm just kind of thinking out loud here", "you can test this"), one joke per note at most, never sarcastic about the person. Address the creator by name when known.
