# Skool About-Page Grading Rubric (2026-09-03-v3)

Only surfaces a creator can edit: display name, sidebar headline (metadata.description, 150-character hard cap), About body (lpDescription, unicode bold allowed, 1,000-character hard cap), cover image, up to six About attachments (video/images). Skool UI, buttons, pricing tiers, trial, reviews-as-a-feature, levels, and join questions are product and are never graded.

## Offer

### R01 (weight 5, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Sell the specific outcome the member will get, not vague access to a community.
**Check:** Check whether the headline or body promises a concrete result (a number, a timeframe, a transformation) rather than just naming a topic or granting access.
**Why:** A confused or generic promise does not convert; buyers act on a stated outcome.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, 10 Important Small-Big Reminders (post); Nick Sarv, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (58:11)

### R02 (weight 4, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Frame the offer as a business or monetary outcome (B2B), not a hobby interest (B2C), when the topic supports it.
**Check:** Check if the headline/body speaks in terms of revenue, clients, or business results versus 'learn about X' hobby language.
**Why:** B2B framing supports higher pricing and lower churn than consumer hobby framing.
**Sources:** Sam Parr (Skool), Skool Games Event Recording Q3 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi (39:20); Nick Sarv, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (125:16)

### R03 (weight 4, partial, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Bundle all courses, trainings, and widgets into one Skool as bonuses of joining rather than selling them separately.
**Check:** Check whether the body references multiple standalone paid products instead of one unified membership with everything included.
**Why:** Selling components separately fragments the offer and confuses buyers.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, 10 Important Small-Big Reminders (post)

### R05 (weight 3, partial, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** State a guarantee or risk-reversal when one exists (money-back, outcome-by-a-date).
**Check:** Check whether the body mentions a guarantee or refund-if-no-result promise.
**Why:** A guarantee removes the buyer's downside and increases conversion on cold traffic.
**Sources:** Nick Saraev, How I won the Skool Games with a $300K/mo, one-man Skool (post)

### R06 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Deliver exactly what the offer promises rather than vague overpromising.
**Check:** Check that claimed deliverables (calls, courses, resources) in the body match what the course/module counts and other fields actually show.
**Why:** Mismatched promises versus delivered substance erode trust and drive refunds.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q3 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi (03:18)

### R07 (weight 3, partial, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Position the community as the product itself ('it's the people'), not just a content library, when peer value is the draw.
**Check:** Check if the body emphasizes the member network and peer access, not only a course catalog.
**Why:** Community-driven value scales without founder time and creates network effects.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q3 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi (01:07)

## Headline

### R08 (weight 5, full, surface: sidebar_headline)
**Rule:** Make the headline state the outcome, not the topic.
**Check:** Check whether the one-line headline names a result ('first 5 clients in 7 days') instead of a subject area ('AI community').
**Why:** Outcome-led headlines convert; topic labels do not tell a stranger why to join.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, 10 Important Small-Big Reminders (post)

### R10 (weight 4, full, surface: sidebar_headline)
**Rule:** Make the headline specific enough that a stranger with zero context understands the niche and promise instantly.
**Check:** Check whether the headline alone (no body needed) is unambiguous about who it's for and what they get.
**Why:** Discovery traffic decides in seconds from the headline and card alone.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Race to $1,000,000/month (07:52); Skool team, How To Get Members (Traffic Sources) (00:00)

### R11 (weight 4, full, surface: sidebar_headline)
**Rule:** Avoid generic marketing slogans in the headline; use concrete, specific language instead.
**Check:** Check the headline for filler phrases ('unlock your potential', 'the best community ever') versus a specific claim.
**Why:** Specificity itself is the selling mechanism; vague hype reads as untrustworthy.
**Sources:** Nick Sarv, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (121:30)

### R57 (weight 4, full, surface: sidebar_headline)
**Rule:** Make the first line of the About body work as a headline a stranger can quote back, since it is the only bold-able line.
**Check:** Read the first line of the body; if it is a greeting, a warning, or a trial notice instead of the outcome, it fails.
**Why:** The first line is what shows above the fold beside the video.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, 10 Important Small-Big Reminders (post)

### R58 (weight 4, full, surface: sidebar_headline)
**Rule:** Keep the sidebar headline (group description) under Skool's 150-character hard cap, aiming for 130-148 so nothing truncates.
**Check:** Count the headline's characters including emojis and spaces. Over 150 fails; a headline that only just squeaks under 150 by dropping the outcome also fails.
**Why:** 150 characters is Skool's real limit on the sidebar line, not a style guess; a punchy outcome has to fit inside it or it gets cut off mid-sentence.
**Sources:** Skool team, Skool NOMAD Framework (doc)

## Body Copy

### R12 (weight 4, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Structure the body around Niche, Outcome, Mechanism, Authority, and Deliverables (the NOMAD shape).
**Check:** Check whether the body covers who it's for, what they get, how it works, why you're credible, and what's included.
**Why:** This is Skool's own tested framework for high-converting About pages.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Race to $1,000,000/month (17:11)

### R13 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Keep the About body focused on outcome and inside Skool's 1,000-character hard cap (aim for 900-985 so nothing gets cut).
**Check:** Count the About body's characters including emojis and line breaks. Over 1,000 fails outright; anything padded with topic rambling instead of outcome language also fails even under the cap.
**Why:** 1,000 characters is Skool's actual limit on the About body, not a style suggestion. A page that reads like 500 words of prose is already over the real ceiling before anyone judges the writing.
**Sources:** Skool team, How To Get Members (Traffic Sources) (00:00); Skool team, Skool NOMAD Framework (doc)

### R14 (weight 5, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** State clearly who the community is for and what shared goal members have.
**Check:** Check for an explicit sentence naming the target member and their shared objective.
**Why:** A page that never names its reader can't make that reader feel seen.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Secret to ENGAGED Communities (09:24)

### R15 (weight 5, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Use specific, concrete pain points and benefits instead of generic promises.
**Check:** Check whether benefit statements name a real, specific problem or number rather than vague uplift language.
**Why:** Specificity is what makes copy believable and self-selecting.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (44:07)

### R16 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Enumerate concrete benefits as a clear list rather than one paragraph of slogans.
**Check:** Check whether the body has a scannable list of what's included/what you get.
**Why:** Skimmable, itemized benefits are easier to evaluate at a glance than dense prose.
**Sources:** Nick Sarv, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (121:30); Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 ([32:30]); Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 (31:55); Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

### R17 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Avoid external links embedded in the About body other than the join action.
**Check:** Check body text for outbound links that could route a prospect off the page before joining.
**Why:** Every external link is a chance to lose the visitor before conversion.
**Sources:** Skool team, Lessons from $100,000/month Skools (09:38); Skool team, Lessons from $100,000/month Skools (09:38); Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

### R18 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Write in plain, simple language, not jargon or dense corporate phrasing.
**Check:** Check whether sentences use a third-grade-level, direct style rather than buzzwords.
**Why:** Plain language that is specific to the audience's problem outsells complex copy.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q2 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + The GOAT (48:58)

### R20 (weight 3, partial, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Describe how the community feels to be in, not only a list of features.
**Check:** Check whether the body conveys tone, belonging, or support in addition to what's included.
**Why:** Buyers join for how it makes them feel as much as for the feature list.
**Sources:** Jack Dumalin, Skool Games Event Recording Q1 2025 (114:12)

### R59 (weight 4, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Never use Skool's banned empty-AI phrases: "No fluff, just results," "Remove the guesswork," "Stop overthinking," "You don't need more ___, you need to know what actually works."
**Check:** Grep the body for these phrases or close paraphrases of them; any match fails.
**Why:** Skool's own classroom lesson flags these exact lines as meaningless filler that reads as generic, not as a promise.
**Sources:** Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

### R60 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Do not include your background or biography on the About page.
**Check:** Check for a standalone paragraph about the creator's history, career, or credentials that isn't in service of a single proof line (see R55).
**Why:** The lesson names biography explicitly as something that does not belong on the page; readers came for the outcome, not the founder's resume.
**Sources:** Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

### R72 (weight 2, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Compact the body for mobile: cut hard line breaks between sections and drop items every Skool member already expects (chat, ask-me-anything access).
**Check:** Check for excess blank-line spacing between sections and for bullets restating platform-default features rather than this community's specifics.
**Why:** Extra whitespace and generic bullets burn scroll distance on a small screen without adding information.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 2 (private Loom) (roast)

## Proof

### R23 (weight 4, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** Back up big claims in the body with visible authority signals (results, credentials, experience).
**Check:** Check whether the body references the founder's track record or specific proof points that support the offer's claims.
**Why:** Authority claims without any visible backing read as unverifiable hype.
**Sources:** Evelyn Weise, Skool Games Event Recording Q1 2025 (36:01); Coach roast, Member About page roast 2 (private Loom) (roast)

### R25 (weight 3, full, surface: about_body)
**Rule:** To stand out, the proof offered should demonstrate real skill and delivery, not just marketing claims.
**Check:** Check whether proof in the body ties back to teaching quality or delivered results, not only follower counts or hype.
**Why:** Being good at the thing and showing it is the actual differentiator, per the Skool team.
**Sources:** Skool team, Skool Games Event Recording Q1 2026 ft. Alex Hormozi + Skool CEO (43:18)

### R61 (weight 4, partial, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Once you have a real testimonial or member win, put it in an attachment slot as a screenshot of the actual post or review.
**Check:** Check whether any attachment shows a real testimonial, review, or member win; a page with reviews available but none surfaced fails.
**Why:** A screenshotted win from inside the community is more convincing proof than a designed testimonial graphic or none at all.
**Sources:** Skool team, He Makes $269k /month on Skool (20:58); Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 (33:39); Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

## Media

### R26 (weight 5, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Attach a native video to the About page.
**Check:** Check whether a video is present on the About page.
**Why:** Native video meaningfully boosts About-page conversion rate.
**Sources:** Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 ([30:51]); Skool team, How To Get Members (Traffic Sources) (00:00)

### R27 (weight 3, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Use the video or cover image to show the topic in action, not a person sitting at a computer.
**Check:** Check whether the attached media depicts the actual activity/outcome rather than a generic desk or talking-head shot.
**Why:** Showing the mechanism in action communicates the niche faster than static or generic imagery.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Secret to ENGAGED Communities (08:16); Skool team, The Race to $1,000,000/month (18:05)

### R28 (weight 3, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Include a real person in the cover image or video rather than only text, logos, or stock graphics.
**Check:** Check whether the cover image/video shows a human face rather than a text-only or abstract graphic.
**Why:** A person in the image communicates the niche and builds personal connection faster than text alone.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Race to $1,000,000/month (16:05)

### R29 (weight 3, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Use the video to peek inside the community (calls, classroom, wins), not just a sales pitch.
**Check:** Check whether the video shows real community moments as evidence rather than only a talking-head pitch.
**Why:** Showing the actual experience is more convincing than describing it.
**Sources:** Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 ([31:22]); Skool team, How To Get Members (Traffic Sources) (20:31)

### R56 (weight 3, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Use up to six attachments (video plus images), each earning its slot, so the About page shows the outcome, the classroom, the community, and wins, not just one talking-head video.
**Check:** Count attachments; fewer than three, or all talking-head, fails.
**Why:** The About page allows six attachments and prospects scroll them like proof.
**Sources:** Skool team, How To Grow Your Skool | Skool News #24 (30:51); Coach roast, Member About page roast 3 (private Loom) (roast)

## Attachments

### R64 (weight 4, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Order attachments by proof: the VSL first, the strongest social proof second, then process or mechanism shots after.
**Check:** Check the attachment sequence; a page that leads with a weak or generic image before the VSL, or buries its best proof at the end, fails.
**Why:** Visitors scroll attachments in order, so the strongest evidence has to front-load the sequence, not trail it.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 1 (private Loom) (roast); Coach roast, Member About page roast 2 (private Loom) (roast)

### R65 (weight 3, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Give each attachment image one idea, not a compound slide trying to make several points at once.
**Check:** Check whether any single attachment stacks multiple unrelated claims or visuals; a slide doing double duty fails.
**Why:** One idea per image is scannable in a half-second scroll; a crowded slide asks the viewer to stop and parse it.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 3 (private Loom) (roast)

### R66 (weight 3, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Do not include an attachment that only restates the cover image's message.
**Check:** Compare each attachment's core message to the cover image; any attachment saying the same thing with no new proof or detail fails.
**Why:** A repeat slide spends one of six scarce slots without adding anything a visitor hasn't already seen.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 2 (private Loom) (roast)

### R67 (weight 3, partial, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Cut any attachment slot that doesn't earn its place; do not fill all six slots by default.
**Check:** Check whether every attachment adds a distinct, load-bearing piece of proof or information; an attachment kept only because the slot exists fails.
**Why:** Having six slots available is not a requirement to use six; a weak filler image dilutes the stronger ones around it.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 3 (private Loom) (roast)

### R68 (weight 3, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Make sure any text inside an attachment image is legible on a mobile screen.
**Check:** Zoom the attachment to roughly a phone's on-screen size; text that's illegible or requires zooming to read fails.
**Why:** Most About-page traffic is mobile, and text too small to read on a phone might as well not be there.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 1 (private Loom) (roast); Coach roast, Member About page roast 3 (private Loom) (roast)

### R69 (weight 2, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Use high-resolution, high-fidelity images and video in every attachment; no visible compression artifacts or blur.
**Check:** Zoom into each attachment; visible pixelation, blur, or resolution mismatch fails.
**Why:** A blurry or low-fidelity image undercuts credibility even when the message itself is right.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 3 (private Loom) (roast)

### R70 (weight 3, partial, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Before you have real reviews or testimonials, leave that slot empty rather than faking or padding it.
**Check:** Check whether a testimonial-shaped attachment actually contains a genuine review; a placeholder, stock quote, or restated cover image standing in for proof fails.
**Why:** A fake or generic stand-in reads worse than no slot at all, and it's easy to add the real thing once it exists.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 2 (private Loom) (roast)

### R71 (weight 3, full, surface: attachments)
**Rule:** Show the actual person and the outcome together in at least one attachment, not only text or product graphics.
**Check:** Check whether at least one attachment shows a real person doing the activity or living the outcome the community teaches.
**Why:** A concrete person-plus-outcome image communicates the promise faster than a bullet list or an abstract graphic.
**Sources:** Skool team, The Race to $1,000,000/month (08:57); Coach roast, Member About page roast 1 (private Loom) (roast)

## Branding

### R51 (weight 5, full, surface: cover_or_copy)
**Rule:** Go as niche and specific as possible rather than describing a broad, generic category.
**Check:** Check whether the headline/body names a narrow, specific niche rather than a broad topic.
**Why:** Narrow niches are more valuable and easier for the right person to instantly recognize as 'for me'.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q1 2026 ft. Alex Hormozi + Skool CEO (17:28); Evelyn Weiss, Skool Games Q1 ft. Evelyn Weiss (04:20)

### R53 (weight 3, partial, surface: cover_or_copy)
**Rule:** Let the founder's real personality and voice come through in the copy rather than generic, interchangeable language.
**Check:** Check whether the body reads as written by a specific person with an opinion, not boilerplate marketing text.
**Why:** Authentic personality is a magnetic differentiator that generic copy cannot fake.
**Sources:** Alex Hormozi, Skool Games Event Recording Q4 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + Skool CEO (14:59)

### R54 (weight 2, full, surface: cover_or_copy)
**Rule:** Keep the display name, headline, and body consistent with each other rather than making conflicting claims.
**Check:** Check whether the name, headline, and body all describe the same niche and promise without contradiction.
**Why:** Consistency across every touchpoint builds recognition; mixed messaging confuses buyers.
**Sources:** Skool CEO, Skool Games Event Recording Q4 2025 ft. Alex Hormozi + Skool CEO (89:51)

### R55 (weight 3, partial, surface: cover_or_copy)
**Rule:** Share one personal credibility line or real setback, not a full background or biography section.
**Check:** Check whether the body has a single proof-bearing personal detail woven into the pitch, versus a standalone 'about me' paragraph or resume-style history.
**Why:** A visible personal stake builds trust, but Skool's own guidance excludes biography from the About page; the story has to work as proof, not as a bio.
**Sources:** Tyler Chen, Skool Games Event Recording Q1 2025 (78:10); Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson)

### R73 (weight 2, full, surface: cover_or_copy)
**Rule:** Avoid the fire, crown, gem, and shamrock emojis in copy or cover text; they read as Skool's own streak and level icons and confuse the message.
**Check:** Scan the cover image, headline, and body for these four emojis; any use fails, unless one is being consciously repurposed for a matching literal meaning (e.g. fire for an actual cooking niche).
**Why:** Those emojis carry a specific platform meaning on Skool (streaks, levels), so reusing them for something else muddies the promise.
**Sources:** Coach roast, Member About page roast 1 (private Loom) (roast)

## Vsl

### R62 (weight 4, partial, surface: media)
**Rule:** Structure the VSL as promise, pain, proof, what's inside, objections, urgency, then a direct call to action.
**Check:** Watch the video and check whether it hits, in roughly this order: what it teaches, the viewer's frustration, evidence it works, what's inside, the top objections answered, a reason to act now, and a plain 'join' ask.
**Why:** This is Skool's own tested VSL structure; skipping a beat (objections especially) leaves a doubt the video never answers.
**Sources:** Skool team, Skool VSL Framework (doc)

### R63 (weight 4, full, surface: media)
**Rule:** Keep the VSL to 2-5 minutes and put enough of the hook in the first 30-40 seconds to hold a distracted viewer.
**Check:** Check the video's runtime and whether the promise or pain point is stated before the 40-second mark; a slow open or a video over 5 minutes fails.
**Why:** Cold traffic decides fast; a video that rambles before it gets to the point loses the viewer before the pitch starts.
**Sources:** Skool team, Platinum classroom: Task 2 About Page (lesson); Coach roast, Member About page roast 1 (private Loom) (roast)
